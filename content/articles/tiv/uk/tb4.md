This past March, my four-year-old daughter came down with a stomach bug that floored her for three solid days. The vomiting, the fever, the sleepless nights — it was a nightmare. Our GP said it was likely a foodborne bacteria she'd picked up right at home.

I was baffled. I keep my kitchen spotless. I'm constantly washing my hands. I cook chicken to exactly the right internal temperature. I just couldn't work out where I'd gone wrong.

Then I came across a study from North Carolina State University on plastic and wooden chopping boards. What I read turned my stomach.

To check if it was true, **I grabbed a hand saw out of the shed and sawed my everyday plastic chopping board right down the middle.**

What I found inside made me chuck the board in the bin that same day.

When I looked at the cross-section of my plastic board, I was horrified. Those faint knife grooves I'd been wiping over every day? They were unbelievably deep. And packed inside them was dark, trapped grime. Moisture, old food residue, and worse.

**Bear in mind: this was a board I scrubbed every single night with washing-up liquid and hot water. Sometimes twice.**

The study I'd read explained exactly what happens. When you use a knife on a plastic board, you carve microscopic cuts into the surface. Within weeks, the board is riddled with thousands of them. Bacteria from raw meat — like salmonella and E. coli — settle deep into those cuts, where a sponge and washing-up liquid physically can't reach them anymore.

But that's not even the most alarming part.

Every time you cut food, your knife scrapes tiny fragments off the plastic board. Researchers found that a single plastic chopping board can release *tens of millions* of microplastic particles per year. You can't see them or taste them, but they end up in every salad, every piece of fruit, every meal you serve your family.

It hit me: I was conscientiously buying healthy food, only to contaminate it on a scratched-up plastic slab.

**Wood and bamboo boards:** They look beautiful, but wood acts like a sponge. It soaks up moisture, chicken juices, and odours. That's why wooden boards still smell of garlic days later. The moisture sinks deep into the fibres and creates a perfect dark environment where mould can grow unnoticed. Plus, they constantly warp and crack.

**Glass boards:** Yes, they're hygienic. But they're so hard they ruined my expensive kitchen knives within a week. I had to press down twice as hard to cut anything, which made my wrists ache and made the knife slip dangerously easily.

I was stuck between a rock and a hard place. Either I was feeding my family bacteria and plastic, or I was wrecking my hands and my knives.
