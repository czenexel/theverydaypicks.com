{{< epic-h2 >}}The Science Every Home Cook Needs to Know{{< /epic-h2 >}}

The research that shook me up so much was published in *Environmental Science & Technology* — one of the most respected scientific journals in the world. Researchers at North Dakota State University tested what happens when you chop food on a regular plastic cutting board.

Their findings were staggering. A single chopping session releases between 1,536 and 7,680 microplastic particles directly into your food. Over a year of regular use, that could mean up to 79.4 million microplastic particles ending up in your body — or about 50 grams of pure plastic.

**What are microplastics?** Microplastics are tiny plastic fragments smaller than 5 millimeters. They form when plastic objects break down through wear and mechanical force — like a knife being dragged across a cutting board thousands of times. Once you ingest them, some can accumulate in your gut, blood, and organs over time.

Then in 2025, researchers at Nanjing University published an even more alarming follow-up study in *Environmental Health Perspectives*. They fed mice food prepared on plastic cutting boards for 12 weeks and watched what happened.

The result? The mice showed significant changes in gut bacteria, altered liver function, and signs of intestinal inflammation. The study concluded that no plastic cutting board should be considered fully "safe."

This isn't fringe theory either. *The New York Times* covered it late last year, and the NDSU research was cited in major medical publications. The science is clear: plastic cutting boards release significant amounts of microplastics into food, and the long-term health implications are concerning.

As a mom who spends an hour a day in the kitchen, it hit me harder than any other health story I've read in years.

{{< epic-h2 >}}Why Switching to Wood or Glass Isn't the Answer{{< /epic-h2 >}}

{{< epic-figure-link image_id="86a60472-8489-44d2-d542-5ee005a0c500" var="variant0001" link="https://track.theverydaypicks.com/click" alt="Tivano titanium cutting board" >}}

My first thought was the obvious one: just grab a wooden board from Williams Sonoma and call it a day.

But once I actually dug in — it turned out every common cutting board material has serious problems.

**Wooden boards** are porous. Bacteria, moisture, and food residue seep into the grain and are nearly impossible to fully sanitize. The knife grooves become breeding grounds for nasty pathogens like salmonella and E. coli. And if you don't oil them regularly, they warp, crack, and start to grow mold — not exactly ideal during humid summer months.

**Bamboo boards** have the same porosity issues, and many are glued together with adhesives that can contain formaldehyde — a known carcinogen.

**Glass boards** are hygienic — but they absolutely destroy your knives. Every cut on glass dulls the blade rapidly, which means less control, more force needed, and constant trips to the knife sharpener. Anyone who's invested in a good set from Wüsthof or Shun knows that pain.
