{{< epic-h2 >}}The ugly truth your plumber never tells you{{< /epic-h2 >}}

Everyone uses their toilet every day. Most people clean it regularly — brush, bleach cleaner, maybe one of those clip-on toilet bowl fresheners under the rim. And then they think: Done.

But what's happening behind the porcelain, where nobody looks? Inside the drain pipes that run from your toilet down into the sewer?

Sullivan describes it like this: "Imagine your blood vessels slowly building up cholesterol. Layer by layer, invisible, painless. Until one day the flow just isn't enough anymore. That's exactly what's happening in your pipes."

Biologists call it **biofilm**. A living layer of bacteria, fungi, calcium, grease, and organic residue. It starts growing from day one and builds up continuously. Every flush pushes water through — but the biofilm clings to the pipe wall like a ring that slowly closes inward.

{{< epic-h2 >}}It's not just clogs — your pipes are a breeding ground for bacteria{{< /epic-h2 >}}

Scientists at the University of Colorado have proven that every toilet flush creates what's known as a "toilet plume." Microscopic water droplets are launched up to 6 feet into the air — carrying bacteria, viruses, and fungal spores that come not just from the bowl, but from the biofilm inside the pipes.

Those particles land on your towel. On your toothbrush. On the floor. On every surface in the bathroom. As long as the biofilm exists in your pipes, every flush is a bacterial shower for your bathroom. Fuugu eliminates the cause — not the symptoms.
