But why does this happen in the first place?

Every time you flush, water flows down through a winding system of pipes. What most people don't realise: not everything drains away completely.

Each time, an invisible layer is left behind on the inner walls of your pipes. Limescale, grease, uric acid, and toilet paper residue. Over the years, this layer grows thicker. We plumbers call it the **"biofilm shield"**.

Think of your pipes like the arteries in your body, and this gunk is like cholesterol. At first, you don't notice a thing. But over months, the flow gets narrower and narrower. Eventually, just a bit more toilet paper is all it takes, and the pipe blocks completely. A heart attack in the bathroom.
