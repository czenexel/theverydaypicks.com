**Not everything flows with it.**

On the inner walls of your drain pipes, every single flush leaves behind a paper-thin layer. Grease, limescale, soap residue, organic matter, cellulose from toilet paper. Individually, barely measurable. But over weeks and months, this layer compacts into a tough, sticky coating.

Dave Barton calls it the **"biofilm armour."**

"Think of your pipes like arteries. And this biofilm is like cholesterol. It builds up layer by layer. Slowly. Invisibly. And eventually, the pipe is so narrowed that nothing can get through."
