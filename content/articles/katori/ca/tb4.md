A few months ago, I came across a study from North Carolina State University that stopped me in my tracks. Researchers found that a single plastic cutting board can release *tens of millions* of microplastic particles per year. Not from misuse. Not from cutting recklessly. From normal, everyday cooking.

Tiny plastic fragments you can't see, can't taste, and can't wash off. They end up in every salad, every piece of fruit, every meal you serve your family.

I looked at the plastic cutting board on my counter – the one I'd been using for years – and felt sick. Then I did something drastic: **I grabbed a hand saw out of the garage and sawed it right down the middle.**

What I found inside made me toss the board in the trash that same day.

When I looked at the cross-section of my plastic board, I was horrified. Those faint knife grooves I'd been wiping over every day? They were unbelievably deep. And packed inside them was dark, trapped grime. Moisture, old food residue, and worse.

**Keep in mind: this was a board I scrubbed every single night with dish soap and hot water. Sometimes twice.**

The study explained exactly what happens. Every time you cut on a plastic board, your knife shaves off microscopic fragments of plastic. Those particles mix invisibly into whatever you're preparing. Your family has been eating them. Meal after meal. Year after year. Microplastics have been found in human blood, lung tissue, and even placentas. And one of the most consistent daily sources? The plastic cutting board sitting on your kitchen counter right now.

But it gets worse. Those same knife cuts also create grooves where bacteria from raw meat – salmonella, E. coli – settle deep inside, where no sponge or dish soap can reach them anymore.

It hit me: I was conscientiously buying healthy food, only to contaminate it with invisible plastic and bacteria on a scratched-up slab.

And then it got personal. A few weeks later, my four-year-old daughter came down with a stomach bug that knocked her flat for three days. Our doctor said it was likely foodborne bacteria picked up at home. I couldn't prove it came from the cutting board. But I couldn't stop thinking about everything I'd just learned.

**Wood and bamboo boards:** They look beautiful, but wood acts like a sponge. It soaks up moisture, chicken juices, and odours. That's why wooden boards still smell like garlic days later. The moisture sinks deep into the fibres and creates a perfect dark environment where mould can grow unnoticed. Plus, they constantly warp and crack. And bamboo boards? They shed their own particles – worn-off varnishes, oils, and protective coatings that end up on your plate just like microplastics do.

**Glass boards:** Yes, they're hygienic and won't shed particles. But they're so hard they ruined my expensive kitchen knives within a week. I had to press down twice as hard to cut anything, which made my wrists ache and made the knife slip dangerously easily.

I was stuck between a rock and a hard place. Either I was feeding my family plastic particles and bacteria, or I was wrecking my hands and my knives.
