I make almost everything from scratch. I buy good cuts of meat, fresh vegetables, and I love having my family over for Sunday dinners. I always thought I was feeding them the healthiest food possible. 

But a few months ago, I was relaxing on the couch with my iPad after dinner. A whistleblower video popped up in my newsfeed. It was recorded by an industry insider—a former materials engineer who used to work for a major plastics manufacturer. 

I clicked play, and within two minutes, I was glued to the screen. 

The insider was explaining how common kitchen items are made, and he held up a small glass jar filled with what looked like white powder. 

"This," he said, pointing to the jar, "is 50 grams of microplastics. It's the exact weight of ten credit cards. And if you use a standard plastic cutting board, this is how much plastic your knife is shaving off and dragging directly into your family's food every single year."

He went on to explain that the industry has known for years that plastic cutting boards cannot withstand sharp chef's knives without shedding microscopic flakes. But because it happens on a microscopic level, no one notices. We just eat it. Over time, those invisible plastics build up in your gut, leading to severe inflammation.
