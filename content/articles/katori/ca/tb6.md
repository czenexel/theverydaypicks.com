Last March, my daughter dropped off my four-year-old granddaughter for the weekend. I was thrilled. I made her favourite fruit salad, sliced up some fresh apples, and we had a wonderful time.

But two days later, my daughter called. My granddaughter had come down with a terrible stomach bug. Vomiting, fever, the whole nightmare. Her pediatrician said it was most likely foodborne bacteria – probably picked up right in my own kitchen.

I was completely baffled. I'm a proud homemaker. I keep my kitchen clean. I wash my hands constantly. I just couldn't figure out where the contamination could have come from.

Then, a few weeks later, my doctor mentioned a new medical article about cutting boards. And what he told me literally turned my stomach.

**The article explained that plastic cutting boards release millions of invisible microplastic particles into your food with every single cut.** Tiny fragments you can't see, can't taste, and can't wash off. They end up in every meal. And I had just sprinkled them right into my granddaughter's fruit salad.

And that's not even the worst part. **The grooves that your knife carves into the plastic surface are pure bacterial highways.** Salmonella, E. coli, and Listeria lodge deep inside those microscopic furrows where no amount of soap, hot water, or scrubbing can ever reach them again. Studies have shown a well-used plastic board can harbour more bacteria per square inch than a toilet seat.

That evening, I went into my kitchen and looked at my plastic cutting board – I mean *really* looked at it. It was covered with deep knife scars. Faded stains from raw meat and vegetables that had never fully come out. A faint garlic smell that lingered no matter how hard I scrubbed.
