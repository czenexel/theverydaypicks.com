## Why your chopping board is contaminating your food (and it's not your fault – it's the material)

Before I tell you more about the Katori, let me briefly explain what Yuki taught me – because once you know this, you'll never look at your kitchen worktop the same way again.

Most chopping boards fall into one of three categories. And every single one of them has a massive problem.

**Plastic boards** are the most common – and the most dangerous. Every time a knife slides across the surface, it cuts a tiny groove into the plastic. Those furrows are invisible to the naked eye at first, but they add up frighteningly fast. Within a few weeks, your board is riddled with thousands of micro-cuts. Bacteria settle into them, multiply, and no amount of thorough washing can ever fully remove them. Worse still: those very same cuts release microplastic particles directly into your food. Current research estimates that a single plastic chopping board can shed tens of millions of microplastic fragments per year – particles that you and your family consume with every meal.

**Wooden boards** seem like the natural alternative, but they have their own hidden problem. Wood is porous. It soaks up moisture, meat juices and odours deep into its fibres. That's why your wooden board still smells of onions three days later – because the wood fibres are literally trapping the smell. That trapped moisture creates the perfect breeding ground for mould and bacteria beneath the surface, completely invisible and completely impossible to clean. On top of that, wooden boards require constant maintenance – oiling, conditioning, careful drying – otherwise they warp, crack or splinter.

**Glass and ceramic boards** do solve the hygiene problem, but they create a new one: they destroy your knives. The surface is so hard that it chips away tiny fragments with every cut and instantly dulls the edge. Within days, your knives are blunt. And a blunt knife, as any chef will tell you, is far more dangerous than a sharp one – because you're forced to press harder, and that's precisely the moment blades slip.

That's the trap most home cooks are stuck in without even realising it. Either you're feeding your family bacteria and plastic, ruining your knives, or both.

The Japanese solved this problem decades ago. They found a material that is non-porous, antibacterial, knife-friendly and virtually indestructible.
