Last March, my four-year-old daughter woke up in the middle of the night being sick. What followed was three solid days of fever, nausea and absolute misery. Our GP said it was almost certainly food-borne bacteria – and that the source was most likely somewhere inside our own kitchen.

I was completely baffled. I keep my kitchen clean. I wash my hands. I cook everything to the right temperature. I just couldn't work out where the contamination could have come from.

Then, a few weeks later, I stumbled across an article about chopping boards. And what I read literally turned my stomach.

The article explained that plastic chopping boards – the exact kind I'd been using for years – release millions of microplastic particles into your food with every single cut. Tiny fragments you can't see, can't taste and can't wash off. They end up in every meal. In every salad. In every piece of fruit you slice up for your kids.

And that's not even the worst part. The grooves that your knife carves into the surface? Those scratches that build up over months of use? They're pure bacterial motorways. Salmonella, E. coli, Listeria – they lodge deep inside those furrows where no amount of washing-up liquid or scrubbing can ever reach them again. Studies have shown that a well-used plastic board can harbour more bacteria per square inch than a toilet seat.

That evening, I went into my kitchen and looked at my chopping board – I mean *really* looked at it – for the very first time. It was covered with deep knife scars. Faded stains from turmeric and beetroot juice that had never fully come out. A faint garlic smell that lingered in the air no matter how hard I scrubbed it.

And I realised: this is the surface I prepare every single meal for my family on.

I threw it in the bin that same night.

## Why Your Chopping Board Is Contaminating Your Food (and It's Not Your Fault – It's the Material)

Before I tell you more about the Katori, let me briefly explain what Yuki taught me – because once you know this, you'll never look at your kitchen worktop the same way again.

Most chopping boards fall into one of three categories. And every single one of them has a massive problem.

**Plastic boards** are the most common – and the most dangerous. Every time a knife slides across the surface, it carves a tiny groove into the plastic. Those furrows are invisible to the naked eye at first, but they add up shockingly fast. Within a few weeks, your board is covered with thousands of micro-cuts. Bacteria nestle into them, multiply, and no amount of thorough washing can ever fully remove them. Worse still: those very same cuts release microplastic particles directly into your food. Recent research estimates that a single plastic chopping board can shed tens of millions of microplastic fragments per year – particles you and your family consume with every meal.

**Wooden boards** seem like the natural alternative, but they have their own hidden problem. Wood is porous. It soaks up moisture, meat juices and odours deep into its fibres. That's why your wooden board still smells of onions three days later – because the wood fibres literally trap the odour. That trapped moisture creates the perfect breeding ground for mould and bacteria beneath the surface, completely invisible and completely impossible to clean. On top of that, wooden boards need constant maintenance – oiling, conditioning, careful drying – or they warp, crack or splinter.

**Glass and ceramic boards** solve the hygiene problem but create a new one: they destroy your knives. The surface is so hard that it chips and blunts the blade with every cut. Within days, your knives are blunt. And a blunt knife, as any chef will tell you, is far more dangerous than a sharp one – because you're forced to press harder, and that's exactly the moment when blades slip.

That's the trap most home cooks are stuck in without even realising it. You're either feeding your family bacteria and plastic, ruining your knives, or both.

The Japanese solved this problem decades ago. They found a material that's non-porous, antibacterial, knife-friendly and virtually indestructible.

That material is titanium. And the <a href="https://track.trendreporter24.com/click" target="_blank">Katori</a> is the first chopping board that brings this technology into your home kitchen.
