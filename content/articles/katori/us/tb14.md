## The Science Every Home Cook Needs to Know

The study that shocked me so deeply was published in *Environmental Science & Technology* — one of the most respected scientific journals in the world. Researchers tested what happens when you chop food on a standard plastic cutting board.

Their findings were staggering. A single chopping session leaves between 1,536 and 7,680 microplastic particles directly on your food. With regular use, this means up to 79.4 million particles enter your body annually — or about 50 grams of pure plastic.

**What are microplastics?** Microplastics are tiny fragments of plastic, smaller than 5 mm. They are created when plastic objects wear down from mechanical force — like a knife gliding over a cutting board thousands of times. Once ingested, they can accumulate in your gut, bloodstream, and organs over time.

In early 2025, researchers published an even more alarming follow-up study in *Environmental Health Perspectives*. They fed mice food prepared on plastic cutting boards for twelve weeks.

The results? The mice showed significant changes in gut bacteria, altered liver functions, and signs of intestinal inflammation. The study concluded that no plastic cutting board can ever be considered completely "safe" again.

This isn't some conspiracy theory. Major news outlets and magazines are now writing extensively about it. The science is clear: plastic cutting boards release massive amounts of microplastics, and the long-term health effects are a serious concern.

As a mom who spends an hour in the kitchen every day, this hit harder than any other health news I've ever read.

## Why Switching to Wood or Glass Isn't The Solution

{{< shortcode-image-no-sub link="https://track.trendreporter24.com/click" image_id="86a60472-8489-44d2-d542-5ee005a0c500" var="variant0001" >}}

My first thought was logical: just buy a wooden board and the problem is solved.

But when I really looked into it, I realized that every common material has its own major issues.

**Wooden boards** are porous. Bacteria, moisture, and food residues seep deep into the grain and are impossible to 100% sanitize. The knife grooves become breeding grounds for bacteria like Salmonella and E. coli. And if you don't oil them regularly, they warp, crack, and start to grow mold.

**Bamboo boards** have the same porosity problems, and many of them are glued together with adhesives that can contain formaldehyde — a known carcinogen.

**Glass boards** are hygienic, that's true — but they destroy your knives. Every single cut on glass rapidly dulls your blade's edge, meaning you have to use more force, knives slip, and you constantly need the sharpener. Anyone with an expensive knife set knows this pain.
