Last March, my four-year-old daughter came down with a gastro bug that knocked her flat for three solid days. The vomiting, the fevers, the sleepless nights — it was a nightmare. Our GP said it was most likely a food-borne bacteria she'd picked up at home.

I was at a loss. I keep my kitchen spotless. I'm constantly washing my hands. I cook chicken to exactly the right internal temperature. I just couldn't work out where I'd slipped up.

Then I came across a study from North Carolina State University on plastic and wooden chopping boards. What I read turned my stomach.

To see for myself if it was really true, **I grabbed a hand saw from the garage and sawed my everyday plastic chopping board straight down the middle.**

What I found inside made me chuck the board in the bin that very same day.

When I looked at the cross-section of my plastic board, I was horrified. Those faint knife grooves I'd been wiping over every single day? They were unbelievably deep. And inside them was dark, trapped grime. Moisture, old food residue, and worse.

**Mind you: this was a board I'd been scrubbing every night with dish soap and hot water. Sometimes twice.**

The study I'd read explained exactly what was going on. Every time you use a knife on a plastic board, you carve microscopic cuts into the surface. Within weeks, the board is riddled with thousands of them. Bacteria from raw meat — like salmonella and E. coli — settle deep inside those cuts, where a sponge and detergent physically can't reach them anymore.

But that's not even the most alarming part.

Every single time you chop food, your knife shaves tiny fragments off the plastic board. Researchers found that a single plastic chopping board can release *tens of millions* of microplastic particles per year. You can't see them, you can't taste them, but they end up in every salad, every piece of fruit, every meal you serve your family.

That's when it hit me: I was carefully buying healthy food, only to contaminate it on a scratched-up plastic slab.

**Wooden and bamboo boards:** They look beautiful, but wood acts like a sponge. It soaks up moisture, chicken juices and odours. That's why wooden boards still smell like garlic days later. The moisture seeps deep into the fibres and creates a perfect dark environment where mould can grow undetected. They also warp and crack constantly.

**Glass boards:** Yes, they're hygienic. But they're so hard they ruined my expensive kitchen knives within a week. I had to press down twice as hard to cut anything, which made my wrists ache and made the knife dangerously easy to slip with.

I was stuck between a rock and a hard place. Either I was feeding my family bacteria and plastic, or I was wrecking my hands and my knives.
